WIDA Standards Alignment: Instructional Evidence

The ENG101 course was designed with explicit WIDA alignment. Below are key instructional moves that address WIDA proficiency bands, particularly targeting students at Developing and Expanding levels.

Day 1-2: Foundational Engagement

Activity: Classmate Bingo, Syllabus Jigsaw, Pair/Share through Responses
WIDA Target: Speaking cluster — Building speaking confidence and peer interaction at Developing level. Students use sentence frames and partner support to discuss course expectations.
ESL Support: Visual jigsaw pieces, word banks, partner scaffolding.

Day 3-4: Rhetorical Analysis Foundations

Activity: Rhetorical Strategies Gallery Walk, Ethos/Pathos/Logos stations, Small Group Annotation
WIDA Target: Reading cluster — Analyzing academic language and persuasive strategies at Expanding level. Students move from identifying terms to explaining their effect.
ESL Support: Colored graphic organizers, sentence starters, visual examples.

Day 5-6: Thesis Building & Writing Frames

Activity: Thesis Workshop, Graphic Organizers, Comparing Voice (Kincaid & Cooper texts)
WIDA Target: Writing cluster — Producing thesis statements with supported evidence at Expanding/Bridging levels. Students use graphic organizers to scaffold argument development.
ESL Support: Thesis sentence frames, evidence scaffolds, peer review protocols.

Day 7-9: Text Analysis & Presentation Skills

Activity: Irony Instruction, Essay Analysis Packet, Slide Etiquette & Presentation Practice
WIDA Target: Speaking cluster — Delivering academic presentations with visual aids at Bridging level. Students practice pacing, emphasis, and eye contact.
ESL Support: Presentation rubric with sentence starters, slide templates, practice with feedback.

Embedded ESL Supports Throughout Curriculum

Exit tickets after each major lesson; vocabulary instruction in context (grouping sounds, syntax practice); sentence frames for discussion and writing; graphic organizers for planning; peer review with sentence starters; repeated reading-aloud practice for fluency development.

Synthesis: Growth from Mid-Year to End-of-Term

1. Academic Confidence Trajectory

Mid-Year: 4.2/10 self-assessment | End-of-Term: 8/10 average performance rating
Interpretation: Students overcame the mid-semester "imposter syndrome" through successful completion of major assignments, positive instructor and liaison feedback, and cumulative mastery of rhetorical concepts.

2. Time Management Remains a Challenge—But Manageable

Mid-Year: 40% cited time management/procrastination as a challenge | End-of-Term: Some improvement but still noted as "neutral" on time management scale
Interpretation: While time management didn't disappear as a concern, students learned to compensate through structured support and clear deadlines. The 6-8 page final essay was cited as overwhelming; breaking into shorter milestones would help.

3. Support Systems Work—Especially Liaisons & Instructor Communication

Finding: 100% of end-of-term respondents reported feeling supported. Liaisons cited instructor communication as crucial.
Interpretation: Consistent touchpoints between school liaisons, instructor, and students created a safety net. This coordination should be formalized in 2026-27 through regular updates and shared dashboards.

4. College Readiness Achievement

Finding: 100% of end-of-term respondents report readiness to take college courses independently, without high school peers.
Interpretation: The HS2EC model successfully bridges the gap. Students entered nervous about college systems and left confident in their ability to navigate college coursework and support resources.

Recommendations for Program Enhancement: 2026-27

  1. Formalize Liaison Communication Protocol: Monthly emails with assignment calendars, student progress flags, and celebration milestones. Use shared Canvas/Dashboard access for real-time updates.
  2. Scaffold Large Writing Assignments: Break the 6-8 page final essay into 3-4 staged submissions: thesis (1 page) → outline with evidence (2 pages) → draft with feedback → final. Reduces overwhelm and increases revision opportunity.
  3. Enhanced WIDA-Aligned Exit Tickets: Continue exit tickets but add optional sentence frames for ESL students to build written expression skills. Track patterns to identify students needing additional writing support.
  4. Technology Onboarding Module: Create a 30-min mandatory orientation covering Canvas, myRCC portal, college email, and troubleshooting. Offer make-up sessions for students who miss the initial session.
  5. Time Management Workshop (Mid-Semester): Partner with student success coach to deliver a 45-min workshop on college study skills, procrastination strategies, and workload balancing. Include time-blocking template.
  6. Peer Mentorship Pairing: Recruit students who successfully completed ENG101 to mentor new HS2EC cohort. Peer mentors model strategies and normalize the college experience.
  7. Expand End-of-Term Survey Sample: Current 4 responses is a small N. Aim for 8-10 end-of-term responses to capture fuller picture. Consider incentives for completion.
  8. Maintain Liaison Visibility & Celebration: Continue the practice of recognizing liaison contributions; include liaison names and quotes in program communications and celebrations.

Conclusion: A Model Program

The RCC Early College Consortium ENG101 course demonstrates that high school students can successfully engage in rigorous college coursework when supported by three pillars: (1) clear, scaffolded instruction with WIDA alignment, (2) consistent liaison and instructor communication, and (3) structured support for college readiness and independence.

Students entered nervous about college systems and abstract rhetorical concepts. Mid-semester, they reported low confidence (4.2/10) and struggled with time management. By end-of-term, they had completed college-level essays, presentations, and assessments — and reported 100% readiness to take future college courses independently.

This program model is replicable, scalable, and worthy of investment. The 2026-27 cohort will benefit from formalized liaison protocols, scaffolded writing assignments, and technology onboarding — changes that cost little but amplify student success.
